How the NBA board works

Each row pairs a posted line with the CGPropz projection, the CG Score and recent hit rates, with the player's game log and matchup context one tap away.

  • Projection: the model's estimate of the player's stat for tonight.
  • CG Score: how far the projection sits from the line. Around 50 means the projection and the line are roughly even, above 50 leans over, and below 50 leans under. A bigger gap from 50 means a bigger gap between the model and the line, and scores on low lines can run higher. How CG Score works.
  • Hit rates: how often the player cleared tonight's line in recent games. The board shows L5, L10, L20, H2H (against tonight's opponent) and Season, with L10 as the main view. Read each with its game count.
  • Line types: The board shows standard lines only.
  • Stat types: Points, Rebounds, Assists, 3-PT Made, Steals, Blocked Shots, Turnovers, Blocks + Steals, Points + Rebounds, Points + Assists, Rebounds + Assists, and Points + Rebounds + Assists.
  • Free preview: none for NBA. The full board is All-Access.

A CG Score is not a probability, and a higher score is not a claim that a prop hits more often.

Reading NBA props early in the season

The first weeks of a season are where hit rates mislead most. Before you lean on a number, check:

  • Small samples. In the opening week, "last five" and "last ten" windows are mostly last season's games, or only a few games from this one. Read the count, not just the percentage.
  • New roles. Trades, signings and rotation changes mean last season's numbers may not describe this season's minutes.
  • Minutes and rest. Early-season minute limits, back-to-backs and late scratches move lines. Check the official injury report before tip-off.
  • Line movement. Lines can move between when the board updates and when you enter. Confirm the current line in your app.
