Your first pick in 10 minutes

Minute 0 to 1: Pick a sport with games today. Open the board for a sport on today's slate. If you're not sure, start with today's free top 3 on the homepage, or sort a board by the L10 hit-rate pill.

Minute 1 to 3: Read one row slowly. Use the example in the next section. Find the line, the projection, the CG Score and the hit rates. Say out loud which side the model leans and why.

Minute 3 to 5: Check the context. Open the player card. Look at the game log, the matchup (defense vs. position), and how many games the hit rates are based on. Check the latest injury and lineup news for both teams.

Minute 5 to 7: Compare the line. Open your pick'em app and find the same player and stat. Is the line the same as on the board? If it moved, the CG Score on the board no longer matches what you'd be picking. Re-check it or skip it.

Minute 7 to 9: Decide the size before the pick. Set a budget for the day first. Start small while you learn. Choose a lineup type you understand (see Power vs Flex below).

Minute 9 to 10: Make the pick, then log it. Write down the player, stat, line, side, CG Score and why you picked it. Reviewing your own picks later teaches you more than any single result.

Passing is a valid outcome. If the line moved, the news changed, or the sample is tiny, skip it.

What CG Score is (and isn't)

CG Score shows how far the CGPropz projection sits from the line. The CGPropz model compares its projection with the posted line and puts the gap on one scale, centered on 50.

  • Around 50: the projection and the line are roughly even.
  • Above 50: the model leans over. Further above means a bigger gap.
  • Below 50: the model leans under. Further below means a bigger gap.

A bigger gap from 50 means a bigger gap between the model and the line. Scores on low lines can run higher. Sports can calculate it a bit differently, and KBO can show its own stored score.

About the free top 3: on NHL and WNBA it's the top three props on the full board by CG Score. KBO's free top 3 is the top three standard lines on the board by CG Score, too. NBA has no free top 3 right now.

The board treats a prop as an over when the projection is at or above the line.

What it doesn't mean: CG Score is not a win probability, and a higher score does not mean a prop is more likely to hit. It tells you where the model and the line disagree most. Your job is to find out why. A very small line can also make a modest projection look like a huge score, so double-check extreme values.

Projections and hit rates

Projection: the model's estimate of a player's stat for today's game, built from recent form, the matchup and the posted line. It's an estimate, not a forecast you can count on.

Hit rate: how often a player cleared today's line over a set of past games. It's always tied to the line. The same player can have a high hit rate at one line and a low one at another.

WindowWhat it coversHow to use it
L5Last five gamesMost current form. Smallest sample, so it swings the most.
L10Last ten gamesA balance of recent and stable. A common default sort.
SeasonEvery game this seasonLargest sample. Slower to reflect role changes.

Which windows appear depends on the sport's board. The NBA board shows L5, L10, L20, H2H and Season, with L10 as the main view. Some boards also show H2H (games against today's opponent), usually the smallest sample of all.

Three rules for reading hit rates:

  1. Count beats percentage. "4 of 5" and "8 of 10" are both 80%, but the second is based on twice the games.
  2. Ask what changed. A new role, an injury to a teammate, or a minutes limit can make old games a poor guide.
  3. Use more than one window. When L5, L10 and season disagree, find out why before picking.

PrizePicks basics: More/Less, goblins and demons

On PrizePicks, a player pick asks one question: will the player go More or Less than the posted projection? A lineup combines several of these picks, and PrizePicks' rules require picks from players on at least two different teams.

Goblins (green icon): an easier version of a projection. Goblins are easier to hit than the standard projection but pay less.

Demons (red icon): a harder version of a projection. Demons are harder to hit but raise the potential payout.

Key points from PrizePicks' own pages:

  • The icon sets the difficulty; More or Less sets the direction. On select projections you can pick either More or Less on a goblin or demon. A Less pick isn't automatically a goblin, and a More pick isn't automatically a demon.
  • You can mix them. A lineup can combine goblins, demons and standard projections.
  • Promotions may not apply. Some promotions exclude goblins and demons. Check each promotion's terms.

How this connects to CGPropz: the board compares the projection with the line it lists. A goblin or demon line is a different number from the standard line, so use the score for the line you're actually picking. Every CGPropz board shows standard lines only. Demon and goblin lines aren't on the boards.

PrizePicks Power Play vs Flex Play

Once you've picked your players, PrizePicks asks how you want the lineup scored:

  • Power Play: every pick in the lineup must be correct to win. In exchange, it carries the higher payout for a given lineup size.
  • Flex Play: the lineup can still pay out if you miss one or two picks, depending on lineup size. In exchange, the top payout is lower than a Power Play of the same size. PrizePicks now offers Flex on two-pick lineups too, where one correct pick returns part of the entry.

Lineups run from two to six picks, and adding picks raises the potential payout. If a player in your lineup doesn't play, PrizePicks may re-grade the lineup at a smaller size under its rules.

Payouts change. PrizePicks says the payout for a specific lineup is the one shown on your lineup screen before you submit. Always check it there. We don't list multipliers on this page because they vary by lineup, picks and promotion.

Which to choose as a beginner: if you're still learning, a Flex Play softens the impact of a single miss, and a smaller lineup is easier to evaluate afterward. Either way, a lineup is only as good as the picks in it.

Underdog and other pick'em apps use similar ideas under different names and rules. Check each app's own help pages.

Glossary

  • All-Access: CGPropz's paid plan with every sport's full board.
  • Board: the list of today's props for a sport, with projections, scores and hit rates.
  • CG Score: how far the CGPropz projection sits from the line. Around 50 means they're roughly even, above 50 leans over, below 50 leans under. It measures the gap, not a probability. Sports can calculate it a bit differently, and KBO can show its own stored score.
  • Demon: on PrizePicks, a harder projection (red icon) with a higher potential payout.
  • DNP: "did not play." Picks on players who don't play are handled under each app's rules.
  • DVP (defense vs. position): how an opponent has defended a given position, used as matchup context.
  • Edge: the gap between a projection and a line. On CGPropz, CG Score expresses it.
  • Flex Play: a PrizePicks lineup type that can still pay out with one or two misses, with a lower top payout.
  • Free top 3: three free props per sport from today's board, shown on the homepage.
  • Game log: a player's game-by-game stats.
  • Goblin: on PrizePicks, an easier projection (green icon) with a lower payout.
  • H2H (head-to-head): a hit rate against today's opponent only.
  • Hit rate: how often a player cleared today's line over a set of past games.
  • L5 / L10: hit rate over the last five or last ten games.
  • Line: the number posted by the pick'em app or sportsbook, for example 18.5 points.
  • Lineup (slip): a set of picks submitted together.
  • More / Less (Over / Under): the direction of a pick relative to the line.
  • Pick'em: fantasy-style contests where you pick More or Less on player stats.
  • Power Play: a PrizePicks lineup type where every pick must be correct, with the higher payout.
  • Projection: the model's estimate of a player's stat for today's game.
  • Push: the result lands exactly on the line. Handling depends on the app's rules.
  • Sample size: how many games a hit rate is based on. Smaller samples swing more.
  • Season hit rate: hit rate over every game this season.
  • Slate: all of today's games for a sport.

FAQ

How do I read player props?

Start with the line and the side, then compare the projection with the line. Check the CG Score, the hit rates and how many games they're based on, then confirm the latest news and the current line in your app.

What is a hit rate in player props?

How often a player cleared today's line over a set of past games, such as the last five, the last ten or the season. Read it with the number of games it covers.

What do goblin and demon mean on PrizePicks?

A goblin (green icon) is an easier projection with a lower payout. A demon (red icon) is a harder projection with a higher potential payout. More or Less still sets the direction of the pick.

What is the difference between Power and Flex on PrizePicks?

In a Power Play every pick must be correct, and the payout is higher. A Flex Play can still pay out with one or two misses, with a lower top payout. Check the exact payout on your lineup screen before you submit.

Does a higher CG Score mean a pick is more likely to hit?

No. CG Score shows how far the projection sits from the line. It is not a probability. Use it to find props worth a closer look.
